          The **Accounting Module** is designed to provide a comprehensive and accurate view of your company's financial health. It enables your business to:

          * **Automate Financial Records**: Capture transactions from Sales and Purchase modules in real-time to maintain an organized General Ledger.
          * **Ensure Compliance**: Manage tax configurations and reporting requirements specific to your operating region, such as Cambodia.

          Accounting in MAQSU ERP follows a synchronized workflow where financial entries are triggered by operational activities across the system.

          ![](/img/accounting/introduction/0001.png)

          2. **Journal Recording**: Transactions are categorized into specific Sales, Purchase, Bank, or Miscellaneous journals.

          4. **Reconciliation**: Matching bank statements with internal records to ensure data integrity.
          5. **Financial Reporting**: Final data is compiled into real-time reports for business analysis.

        title: Create Invoice
        body: |

          An **Invoice** is an official document sent to a customer that details goods or services provided, their quantities, unit prices, taxes, and the total amount due. In MAQSU ERP, invoices are crucial for tracking sales, managing customer payments, and ensuring proper financial records.

          > **Note:** Invoices are generally created after confirming and delivering a sales order, or you may generate a customer invoice for direct sales transactions. Accurate invoicing is vital for compliance and cash flow.

          ---

          ### Step 1: Go to Customer Invoices

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** → **Invoices**.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0001.png)

          ---

          ### Step 2: Invoice List Overview

          Once you navigate to **Invoices**, you will see the master list of all existing customer invoices. The list displays the following columns:

          1. **Number** – The unique invoice number automatically assigned to each invoice (e.g., INV/2026/0005).
          2. **Reference** – An optional internal reference linked to the invoice.
          3. **Invoice Date** – The date the invoice was issued.
          4. **Customer** – The name of the customer receiving the invoice.
          5. **Due Date** – The payment due date based on the payment terms.
          6. **Total** – The total invoice amount including taxes.
          7. **Amount Due** – The remaining outstanding balance to be paid.
          8. **Status** – The current document status, either **Draft** or **Posted**.
          9. **Payment** – The payment status, either **Paid**, **Not Paid**, or **Partial**.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0002.png)

          ---

          ### Step 3: Click Create

          Click the **Create** button at the top-left of the Invoices list page to open a new blank invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0003.png)

          ---

          ### Step 4: Fill Out the Invoice Form

          The new invoice form will open in **Draft** status. Fill in the following required and optional fields:

          #### Header Fields

          1. **Customer** *(Required)* – Search and select the customer by name, code, email, or reference. Start typing and select the correct entry from the dropdown list.

          2. **Reference** *(Optional)* – Enter an internal reference number or note to help identify this invoice. This is useful for linking the invoice to a sales order or project.

          3. **Invoice Date** – The date the invoice is issued. This defaults to today's date but can be adjusted.

          4. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system after saving. You do not need to fill this in manually.

          5. **Payment Terms** – Select the agreed payment timeframe or conditions (e.g., 15 Days, Due on Receipt). This determines the due date shown on the invoice.

          6. **Journal** – The accounting journal where the invoice will be posted. This defaults to **Customer Invoices (USD)**.

          7. **Pricelist** *(Optional)* – Select a pricelist to automatically apply special pricing or discounts for the customer. The selected pricelist will update product prices on the invoice lines accordingly.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0004.png)

          Once you fill in the customer and other header details, the form will update to show the entered information.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0005.png)

          ---

          ### Step 5: Add Products or Services

          Under the **Invoice Lines** tab, add the products or services you are invoicing the customer for. Click **Add a line** to add a new line item, then fill in the following fields for each line:

          1. **Product** – Choose the product or service being invoiced. Start typing the product name and select the correct entry from the list of created products in **Inventory > Products**. This will auto-populate the description, account, and price. For more information on creating products, see [Create Product Guide](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#products).

          2. **Description** – Optionally modify or add a detailed description for the item. This appears on the printed invoice sent to the customer.

          3. **Account** – The accounting account used to classify this revenue line (e.g., 401100 Sales of Goods). This determines how the sale is tracked in financial reports.

          4. **Quantity** – The number of units being invoiced. The subtotal updates automatically.

          5. **UOM** – The unit of measure for the item (e.g., Units, Can, kg).

          6. **Price** – The unit price per item. This may auto-fill from the product or pricelist but can be adjusted.

          7. **Discount** – Enter a percentage discount to apply on this line. The subtotal will update to reflect the discount.

          8. **Taxes** – Select the applicable taxes for the product (e.g., VAT - Sales of Goods 10%). The tax amount is calculated and included in the total.

          9. **Subtotal** – Automatically calculated as: **Subtotal = (Quantity × Price) – Discount**.

          You can also use **Add a section** to group related items, or **Add a note** to include internal remarks on a specific line.

          At the bottom of the Invoice Lines section, the **Total Summary** shows:
          - **Untaxed Amount** – The sum of all line subtotals before taxes.
          - **VAT** – The total tax amount applied.
          - **Total** – The grand total due including taxes.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0006.png)

          <Noted type="info" title="Note" image="/img/icon/notes.png">
          Carefully verify that all amounts are correct. If any discrepancies are found, review and adjust the corresponding invoice lines or tax settings before continuing.
          </Noted>

          ---

          ### Step 6: Save the Invoice

          After completing all required fields, click **Save** to save the invoice as a **Draft**. At this stage the invoice is not yet posted to accounting — you can still edit it.

          ---

          ### Step 7: Click Post to Confirm the Invoice

          Once you have reviewed and confirmed all details are correct, click the **Post** button to officially post the invoice. This will:

          - Change the invoice status from **Draft** to **Posted**
          - Assign a permanent invoice number (e.g., INV/2026/0007)
          - Record the corresponding journal entries in accounting

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0007.png)

          After posting, the invoice is locked for editing. You will see the **Add Payment**, **Add Credit Note**, and **Reset to Draft** buttons at the top of the form.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0008.png)

          <Noted type="info" title="Note" image="/img/icon/notes.png">
          If you notice an error after posting, click **Reset to Draft** to bring the invoice back to Draft status for editing, then re-post it once corrections are made.
          </Noted>

          ---

          For more details on invoice management, see the [Customer Invoice Guide](/accounting/create-customer-invoice).

      - link: /payment-invoice
        title: Add Payment to Invoice
        body: |

          A **Payment** is a record in MAQSU ERP that tracks money received from a customer against a posted invoice. Registering a payment correctly ensures your accounting records are accurate, your invoices are marked as settled, and your cash flow is properly reflected.

          > **Note:** A payment can only be registered against a **Posted** invoice. Always verify the invoice details before registering a payment.

          ---

          In MAQSU ERP, there are **two ways** to register a customer payment:

          * **Option 1 — From the Invoice:** Register payment directly on a posted customer invoice using the **Add Payment** button.
          * **Option 2 — From the Payments Menu:** Create a standalone payment record from the Customers → Payments menu and link it to an outstanding invoice.

          ---

          ### Option 1: Add Payment Directly from Invoice

          This is the quickest method. It automatically pre-fills the payment amount and links it directly to the invoice being paid.

          #### Step 1: Go to Customer Invoices

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** → **Invoices**.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0001.png)

          ---

          #### Step 2: Find and Open the Invoice

          Use the **Search bar** to find the invoice you want to register payment for. Type the invoice number (e.g., INV/2026/0007) and select **Search Invoice for: INV/2026/0007** from the dropdown suggestions.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0002.png)

          The list will filter to show the matching invoice with the **Payment** status showing **Not Paid**. Click on the **Invoice Number** to open it.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0003.png)

          ---

          #### Step 3: Click Add Payment

          On the posted invoice, click the **Add Payment** button at the top of the invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0004.png)

          An **Add Payment** dialog will appear. Fill in the following details:

          1. **Journal** – Select the payment account where the money is received (e.g., Bank (USD), Cash). This determines which financial account is credited.
          2. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system. Leave as is or enter a custom reference such as a bank transfer ID or check number.
          3. **Amount** – Pre-filled with the outstanding invoice balance. Adjust for partial payments if needed.
          4. **Payment Date** – The date the payment was received from the customer. Defaults to today.
          5. **Memo** – Auto-populated with the invoice number for reference (e.g., INV/2026/0007).

          Click **Validate** to confirm and post the payment.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0005.png)

          ---

          #### Step 4: Verify the Invoice is Marked as PAID

          After validating, the invoice will be updated immediately. A green **PAID** stamp will appear on the top-right corner of the invoice form, confirming the invoice has been fully settled and the payment has been recorded in your accounting.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0006.png)

          <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
          If a partial payment was registered, the invoice will show **Partially Paid** instead of **PAID**. You can click **Add Payment** again to register the remaining balance until the invoice is fully settled.
          </Noted>

          ---

          ### Option 2: Register Payment from the Payments Menu

          This method allows you to create a standalone payment record first and then match it to one or more outstanding invoices. It is useful when you receive a payment from a customer before an invoice is created, or when matching payments to multiple invoices.

          #### Step 1: Go to Payments

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** → **Payments**.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0007.png)

          ---

          #### Step 2: Payments List Overview

          The Payments list shows all existing customer payment records. The list displays the following columns:

          1. **Number** – The unique payment number assigned to each record (e.g., CUST.IN/2026/0009). Outgoing refund payments are prefixed with **CUST.OUT**.
          2. **Payment Date** – The date the payment was made.
          3. **Customer** – The customer who made the payment.
          4. **Journal** – The bank or cash account the payment was received into.
          5. **Amount** – The total payment amount.
          6. **Status** – The current status of the payment: **Validated** (posted but not yet matched to an invoice) or **Reconciled** (matched and settled against an invoice).

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0008.png)

          ---

          #### Step 3: Click Create

          Click the **Create** button at the top-left of the Payments list page to open a new blank payment form.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0009.png)

          ---

          #### Step 4: Fill in the Payment Form

          The new payment form will open with a document number assigned (e.g., CUST.IN/2026/0011). Fill in the following fields:

          1. **Payment Type** – Select the direction of the payment:
              - **Receive Money** – Use this for incoming payments from customers *(default for customer payments)*.
              - **Send Money** – Use this for outgoing payments or refunds to customers.
              - **Internal Transfer** – Use this for transfers between your own bank accounts.

          2. **Journal** – Select the bank or cash account the payment is received into. Available options include Bank (USD), AC Bank (USD), Philip Bank (USD), Woori (USD), KB Bank (USD), ABA Bank (USD), and Cash (USD).

          3. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system.

          4. **Partner** – Search and select the customer making the payment.

          5. **Amount** – Enter the payment amount received from the customer.

          6. **Payment Date** – The date the payment was received. Defaults to today.

          7. **Memo** – Enter an optional internal note or reference, such as the invoice number the payment relates to.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0010.png)

          ---

          #### Step 5: Click Confirm and Match to Invoice

          After filling in all required fields, click **Confirm** to post the payment. A **Payment Matching** dialog will appear automatically, showing a list of outstanding invoices for the same customer that can be matched against this payment.

          Review the list and select the invoice(s) you want to match. The dialog shows:
          - **Date** – The invoice date.
          - **Due Date** – The invoice payment due date.
          - **Number** – The invoice number (e.g., INV/2026/0007).
          - **Total** – The invoice total amount.
          - **Amount Due** – The remaining outstanding balance on the invoice.

          Once you have selected the correct invoice(s), click **Validate** to confirm the matching and reconcile the payment against the invoice. You can also click **Skip and Post** to post the payment without matching it to any invoice at this time.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0011.png)

          ---

          #### Step 6: Verify Payment Status is Validated

          After confirming, the payment record will update to **Validated** status. The payment detail page (e.g., CUST.IN/2026/0011) will display:

          - **Payment Type** – Receive Money
          - **Journal** – The bank account used (e.g., Bank (USD))
          - **Partner** – The customer name
          - **Amount** – The payment amount
          - **Payment Date** – The date of payment
          - **Memo** – The reference note

          You will also see two smart buttons at the top right: **Journal Items** (to view the accounting entries) and **1 Invoice** (to view the matched invoice).

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0012.png)

          ---

          #### Step 7: (Optional) Print Receipt or Voucher

          After the payment is validated, you can print a receipt or voucher for the customer. Click the **Print** button at the top of the payment form and select the appropriate document:

          - **Receipt / Payment Voucher** – Prints a standard payment receipt to give to the customer.
          - **Deposit Receipt** – Prints a deposit receipt if the payment is a deposit or advance.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0013.png)

          <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
          **Option 1** (from the invoice) is recommended for straightforward cases where you are registering a payment for a single specific invoice. **Option 2** (from the Payments menu) is better suited for advance payments, bulk matching, or when you need more control over the payment journal and matching process.
          </Noted>

          A **Credit Note** is an official document issued to a customer to reverse or reduce the amount owed from a previously posted invoice. In MAQSU ERP, credit notes are used to handle refunds, billing corrections, or returned goods. They are recorded in accounting and directly reduce the outstanding balance on the original invoice.

          > **Note:** A credit note can only be created after the original invoice has been posted. Always reference the original invoice number when creating a credit note to maintain accurate financial records.

          ---

          In MAQSU ERP, there are **two ways** to issue a Credit Note to a customer:

          * **Option 1 — From the Credit Notes Menu:** Create a credit note directly from the Credit Notes list under the Customers menu.
          * **Option 2 — From the Invoice:** Add a credit note directly from a posted customer invoice using the **Add Credit Note** button.

          ---

          ### Option 1: Create Credit Note from Credit Notes Menu

          #### Step 1: Go to Credit Notes

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** → **Credit Notes**.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0001.png)

          ---

          #### Step 2: Click Create

          The Credit Notes list page will open. If no credit notes have been created yet, the page will show an empty state with the message *"The easiest way to create a credit note is to do it directly from the customer invoice."*

          Click the **Create** button at the top-left to open a new blank Credit Note form.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0002.png)

          ---

          #### Step 3: Fill Out the Credit Note Form

          The new credit note form will open in **Draft** status. Fill in the following fields:

          1. **Customer** *(Required)* – Search and select the customer this credit note is issued for.
          2. **Reference** *(Optional)* – Enter an internal reference or reason for the credit note (e.g., "Refund to Long Meth").
          3. **Invoice No. Ref** – Enter the original invoice number this credit note is linked to (e.g., INV/2026/0007). This links the credit note to the source invoice for traceability.
          4. **Invoice Date Ref** – Select the date of the original invoice being referenced.
          5. **Invoice Date** – The date this credit note is issued. Defaults to today.
          6. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system after saving.
          7. **Payment Terms** – The payment terms carried over from the original invoice.
          8. **Journal** – The accounting journal to post this credit note to, defaulting to **Customer Invoices (USD)**.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0003.png)

          Once filled in, the form will show all the entered header details.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0004.png)

          ---

          #### Step 4: Add Invoice Lines

          Under the **Invoice Lines** tab, add the products or services being credited. Click **Add a line** and fill in the Product, Description, Account, Quantity, UOM, Price, Discount, and Taxes for each line.

          The **Total Summary** at the bottom displays:
          - **Untaxed Amount** – Sum of all line subtotals before taxes.
          - **VAT** – The total tax amount applied.
          - **Total** – The grand total of the credit note including taxes.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0005.png)

          ---

          #### Step 5: Save and Click Post

          After completing all fields, click **Save** to save the credit note as a Draft. Then click **Post** to officially post the credit note.

          This will change the status from **Draft** to **Posted** and assign a permanent credit note number (e.g., RINV/2026/0001).

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0006.png)

          ---

          #### Step 6: Register Refund Payment

          Once posted, the credit note number is assigned (e.g., **RINV/2026/0001**). Click **Add Payment** to register the refund payment back to the customer.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0007.png)

          An **Add Payment** dialog will appear. Fill in the following:

          1. **Journal** – Select the payment account the refund is paid from (e.g., Bank (USD)).
          2. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated. Leave as is or enter a custom reference.
          3. **Amount** – Pre-filled with the credit note total (e.g., $33.26).
          4. **Payment Date** – The date the refund was issued to the customer.
          5. **Memo** – Auto-populated from the credit note reference (e.g., "Refund to Long Meth").

          Click **Validate** to confirm and post the refund payment.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0008.png)

          ---

          #### Step 7: Verify Credit Note is Marked as PAID

          After validating, the credit note will be stamped with a green **PAID** banner confirming the refund has been fully processed. The **Amount Due** at the bottom will show **$0.00** and the **Payment Info** section will display the refund details including the amount, memo, date, and payment journal.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0009.png)

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0010.png)

          ---

          ### Option 2: Add Credit Note Directly from Invoice

          This is the quickest and recommended method as it automatically links the credit note to the original invoice and pre-fills all the relevant details.

          #### Step 1: Go to Customer Invoices

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** → **Invoices**.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0011.png)

          ---

          #### Step 2: Find the Invoice

          Use the **Search bar** to find the invoice you want to issue a credit note for. Type the invoice number (e.g., INV/2026/0007) and select **Search Invoice for: INV/2026/0007** from the dropdown suggestions.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0013.png)

          ---

          #### Step 3: Click Add Credit Note

          On the posted invoice (status **Posted** / **PAID**), click the **Add Credit Note** button at the top of the invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0014.png)

          A **Draft Credit Note** will be automatically created and pre-filled with all the details from the original invoice, including the customer, invoice lines, taxes, and a reference to the original invoice number. The **Journal** will be set to **Customer Refund (USD)**.

          Click **Post** to officially post the credit note.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0016.png)

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0017.png)

          ---

          #### Step 5: Verify Credit Note is Marked as PAID

          After validating, the credit note will be stamped with a green **PAID** banner, confirming the refund has been fully processed and the credit note is settled.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0018.png)

            </Noted>

            Printing an invoice in this system is a straightforward process. Here is the breakdown of how to do it:

          #### Frequently Asked Questions

          ##### Can I upload multiple bills at once?
          Yes, you can select and upload multiple bill files in one batch. Each will be processed separately and you'll review them one at a time before posting.

          ##### What if the AI extracts incorrect information?
          You can manually correct any field before posting. Click on the field and edit the value directly. Always review the extracted data carefully against the original document.

          ##### Can I save a partially reviewed bill?
          Yes, you can save the bill as **Draft** status and come back to it later for final review and posting. This is useful for bills that need manager approval or additional verification.

          ##### What happens to the original bill file?
          The uploaded file is automatically attached to the bill record in MAQSU. You can view or download it anytime from the bill page for reference or audit purposes.

          ##### Does this work with bills in other languages?
          AI recognition accuracy depends on the language and character set. English bills typically have the highest accuracy. Contact support for information about specific language support.

          ##### Can I use this for customer invoices too?
          Yes, MAQSU ERP also has an **AI Invoice Upload** feature for customer invoices. The process is similar but creates customer invoices instead of vendor bills.

          ##### What if my bill has a non-standard format?
          The AI is trained on common bill and invoice formats. Unusual or highly customized layouts may require more manual correction during review. The system will still capture as much data as possible.

          Upload all pages of the bill as a single PDF file, or take photos of each page and combine them before uploading. The AI will process the complete document.

          ### Frequently Asked Questions

          #### What if I forgot to assign an asset category?

          If you posted a bill without an asset category, the purchase was recorded as an expense instead of an asset. To fix:
          1. Reset the bill to draft (if no payment made)
          2. Edit the line item and add the asset category
          3. Re-post the bill
          4. Or create a journal entry to reclassify from expense to asset

          #### Can I add the asset category after posting?

          No, you must reset the bill to draft first, add the category, then re-post. If a payment has been made, you'll need to create correcting journal entries.

          #### What if the asset category doesn't exist?

          You can create it on the fly:
          1. In the Asset Category dropdown, select "Create and Edit..."
          2. Fill in the category details
          3. Save and return to the bill
          4. The new category is now available

          #### Do I include shipping and installation costs?

          Yes! Asset cost should include ALL costs necessary to get the asset ready for use:
          * Purchase price
          * Shipping and delivery
          * Installation and setup
          * Initial training (if asset-specific)
          Add these as separate line items on the bill, all with the same asset category.

          #### What about asset maintenance or repairs?

          Maintenance and repairs are expensed immediately, NOT capitalized as assets. Only use asset categories for:
          * Original purchase
          * Major improvements that extend useful life
          * Major upgrades that increase capacity

          Regular maintenance goes to expense accounts.

          #### Can I purchase an asset without a vendor bill?

          Technically yes (using journal entries), but it's not recommended. Always create a vendor bill for asset purchases to:
          * Maintain vendor payment records
          * Track accounts payable
          * Provide audit trail
          * Link to purchase documents

          #### What if I pay cash immediately?

          Create the vendor bill as normal with the asset category. Then immediately add a payment:
          1. Post the bill
          2. Click "Add Payment"
          3. Select Cash as the journal
          4. Post the payment
          This maintains proper accounting records.

          By properly recording asset purchases with asset categories, you ensure accurate financial reporting, automate depreciation tracking, and maintain complete records of your company's fixed assets from acquisition through disposal.

              ### What is an Asset Category?

              An **Asset Category** is a template that defines:

              #### Depreciation Settings
              * **Computation Method** – How depreciation is calculated (Straight Line or Declining Balance)
              * **Period Type** – Whether depreciation is calculated based on number of entries, ending date, or yearly
              * **Number of Depreciations** – How many periods the asset will be depreciated over
              * **Period Interval** – How often depreciation entries are created (monthly, quarterly, yearly)

              #### Accounting Configuration
              * **Journal** – Which journal to use for depreciation entries
              * **Asset Account** – Where the asset value is recorded on the balance sheet
              * **Depreciation Account** – Where accumulated depreciation is tracked
              * **Expense Account** – Where depreciation expense is recorded on the income statement

              #### Automation Options
              * **Auto-Confirm Assets** – Automatically confirm assets when created
              * **Group Journal Entries** – Combine multiple depreciation entries into one journal entry
              * **Split Monthly Entry** – Create separate entries for each month
              * **Depreciation Date** – When to start depreciation (purchase date or manual)
              * **Accounting Date** – When to record depreciation in accounting books

              By setting up asset categories properly, you can ensure accurate and consistent depreciation accounting across all your fixed assets.

              ### Why Asset Categories Are Important?

              * Define depreciation methods and rates for each asset type
              * Ensure consistent accounting treatment for similar assets
              * Automate depreciation calculations and journal entries
              * Organize assets by type for better tracking and reporting
              * Simplify asset setup—just select a category instead of configuring each asset individually

              **Common Asset Categories:**
              * Computer Equipment (laptops, desktops, servers)
              * Office Furniture (desks, chairs, cabinets)
              * Vehicles (cars, trucks, delivery vans)
              * Machinery and Equipment (production equipment)
              * Buildings and Property
              * Leasehold Improvements


              ### Straight Line vs Declining Balance

              #### Straight Line Depreciation

              **How it works:**
              * Same amount of depreciation each period
              * Most common and simplest method
              * Matches consistent use pattern of assets

              **Formula:**
              ```
              Annual Depreciation = (Asset Cost - Salvage Value) ÷ Useful Life in Years
              ```

              **Example:**
              * Asset Cost: $10,000
              * Salvage Value: $1,000 (value at end of life)
              * Useful Life: 5 years
              * Annual Depreciation: ($10,000 - $1,000) ÷ 5 = $1,800 per year
              * Monthly Depreciation: $1,800 ÷ 12 = $150 per month

              **Depreciation Schedule:**
              | Year | Beginning Value | Depreciation | Ending Value |
              |------|----------------|--------------|--------------|
              | 1 | $10,000 | $1,800 | $8,200 |
              | 2 | $8,200 | $1,800 | $6,400 |
              | 3 | $6,400 | $1,800 | $4,600 |
              | 4 | $4,600 | $1,800 | $2,800 |
              | 5 | $2,800 | $1,800 | $1,000 |

              **Journal Entry Each Period:**
              ```
              Debit: Depreciation Expense    $150
              Credit: Accumulated Depreciation    $150
              ```

              ---

              #### Declining Balance Depreciation

              **How it works:**
              * Higher depreciation in early years
              * Depreciation amount decreases over time
              * Reflects faster loss of value for some assets

              **Formula:**
              ```
              Depreciation = Current Book Value × Depreciation Rate
              ```

              **Common rates:**
              * 20% (5-year asset, 200% declining balance)
              * 40% (2.5-year asset, 200% declining balance)

              **Example:**
              * Asset Cost: $10,000
              * Depreciation Rate: 20%
              * Method: Double Declining Balance

              **Depreciation Schedule:**
              | Year | Beginning Value | Depreciation (20%) | Ending Value |
              |------|----------------|-------------------|--------------|
              | 1 | $10,000 | $2,000 | $8,000 |
              | 2 | $8,000 | $1,600 | $6,400 |
              | 3 | $6,400 | $1,280 | $5,120 |
              | 4 | $5,120 | $1,024 | $4,096 |
              | 5 | $4,096 | $819 | $3,277 |

              **When to use:**
              * Technology assets (computers, phones)
              * Vehicles
              * Equipment that loses value quickly
              * When tax benefits are important

          1.  **Not Due** – Bills that have been recorded but have not yet reached their payment due date.
          2.  **Aging Buckets (1 - 180+)** – Outstanding balances categorized by the number of days they are past due (e.g., 1-30 days, 31-60 days).
          3.  **Total** – The cumulative outstanding balance owed to each specific vendor.
