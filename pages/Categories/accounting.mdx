          Accounting in MAQSU follows a synchronized workflow where financial entries are triggered by operational activities across the system.

          ![](/img/accounting/introduction/0002.png)

          1. **Transaction Entry**: Triggered by Sales Invoices, Vendor Bills, or manual Journal Entries.
          2. **Journal Recording**: Transactions are categorized into specific Sales, Purchase, Bank, or Miscellaneous journals.
          3. **General Ledger Posting**: Entries are automatically mapped to the appropriate accounts in the Chart of Accounts.
          4. **Reconciliation**: Matching bank statements with internal records to ensure data integrity.
          5. **Financial Reporting**: Final data is compiled into real-time reports for business analysis.

          The **Invoices** feature in MAQSU allows you to create and manage invoices for your customers. Let's explore how to create a new customer invoices in MAQSU:

          ---

          ### Create Invoice

          To create a new customer invoice, follow the steps below:

          ---

          ##### Step 1 — Navigation

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** ‣ **Invoices**.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0001.png)

          Once you navigate to **Invoices**, you will see the master list of all existing customer invoices. The list displays the following columns:

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0002.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 1 — Create

          Click the **Create** button at the top-left of the Invoices list page to open a new blank invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0003.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 2 — Basic information

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0005.png)

          The new invoice form will open in **Draft** status. Fill in the following required and optional fields:
          1. **Customer** _(Required)_ – Search and select the customer by name, code, email, or reference. Start typing and select the correct entry from the dropdown list. Let's see how to setup [Cusomters](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#customer-vendor)
          2. **Reference** _(Optional)_ – Enter an internal reference number or note to help identify this invoice. This is useful for linking the invoice to a sales order or project.
          3. **Invoice Date** – The date the invoice is issued. This defaults to today's date but can be adjusted.
          4. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system after saving. You do not need to fill this in manually.
          5. **Payment Terms** – Select the agreed payment timeframe or conditions (e.g., 15 Days, Due on Receipt). This determines the due date shown on the invoice. Let's see how to setup [Payment Terms](/accounting/configurations#payment-terms)
          6. **Journal** – The accounting journal where the invoice will be posted. This defaults to **Customer Invoices (USD)**. Let's see how to setup [Journals](/accounting/configurations#journals)
          7. **Pricelist** _(Optional)_ – Select a pricelist to automatically apply special pricing or discounts for the customer. The selected pricelist will update product prices on the invoice lines accordingly.

          Once you fill in the customer and other header details, the form will update to show the entered information.

          ---

          ##### Step 3 — Add products or services

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0006.png)

          Under the **Invoice Lines** tab, add the products or services you are invoicing the customer for. Click **Add a line** to add a new line item, then fill in the following fields for each line:

          1. **Product** *(Optional)* – Choose the product or service being invoiced. Typing the product name and select the correct entry from the list of created products. This will auto-populate the description, account, and price. For more information on creating products, see how to [Create Product](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#products).
          2. **Description** *(Optional)* – Optionally modify or add a detailed description for the item. This appears on the printed invoice sent to the customer.
          3. **Account** *(Required)* – The accounting account used to classify this revenue line (e.g., 401100 Sales of Goods). This determines how the sale is tracked in financial reports.
          4. **Quantity** *(Required)* – The number of units being invoiced. The subtotal updates automatically.
          5. **UOM** *(Required)* – The unit of measure for the item (e.g., Units, Can, kg).
          6. **Price** *(Required)* – The unit price per item. This may auto-fill from the product or pricelist but can be adjusted.
          7. **Discount** *(Optional)* – Enter a percentage discount to apply on this line. The subtotal will update to reflect the discount.
          8. **Taxes** *(Optional)* – Select the applicable taxes for the product (e.g., VAT - Sales of Goods 10%). The tax amount is calculated and included in the total.
          9. **Subtotal** *(Auto)* – Automatically calculated as: **Subtotal = (Quantity x Price) – Discount**.

          You can also use **Add a section** to group related items, or **Add a note** to include internal remarks on a specific line.

          At the bottom of the Invoice Lines section, the **Total Summary** shows:

          - **Untaxed Amount** – The sum of all line subtotals before taxes.
          - **VAT** – The total tax amount applied.
          - **Total** – The grand total due including taxes.

          ---

          ##### Step 5 — Post

           Click **Save** to save the invoice as a **Draft** status, allowing you to still make edits. Once you have carefully reviewed and confirmed all details are correct, click **Post** to officially post the invoice. This will:

          - Change the invoice status from **Draft** to **Posted** – Assign a permanent invoice number (e.g., INV/2026/0007)
          - Record the corresponding journal entries in accounting

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0007.png)

          After posting, the invoice is locked for editing. You will see the **Add Payment**, **Add Credit Note**, and **Reset to Draft** buttons at the top of the form. Let's see how to add payment for the posted cusotmer invoice [Add Payment](#account-receivable-invoices-addpayment).

          <Noted type="info" title="Note" image="/img/icon/notes.png">
            If you notice an error after posting, click **Reset to Draft** to bring the invoice back to
            Draft status for editing, then re-post it once corrections are made.
          </Noted>

          ---

          ### Add Payment

          Once the customer gets this invoice, they can check the details and pay the invoice. So now you can record their payment using the **Add Payment** button.

          ---

          ##### Step 1 — Add payment

          On the posted invoice, click the **Add Payment** button at the top of the invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0004.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 2 — Add payment details

          MAQSU generates a pop-up window to confirm the details of the payment registration. The window will show the:

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0005.png)

          1. **Journal** *(Required)* – Select the payment account where the money is received (e.g., Bank (USD), Cash). This determines which financial account is credited.
          2. **Doc. No.** *(Auto)* – Automatically generated by the system. Leave as is or enter a custom reference such as a bank transfer ID or check number.
          3. **Amount** *(Auto)* – Pre-filled with the outstanding invoice balance. Adjust for partial payments if needed.
          4. **Payment Date** *(Auto)* – The date the payment was received from the customer. Defaults to today.
          5. **Memo** *(Auto)* – Auto-populated with the invoice number for reference (e.g., INV/2026/0007).

          You can make changes to the available details if needed. Now, click the **Validate** button. The payment registration can be completed by this step. Once the customer pays the invoice, the status of the invoice can be observed as Paid.

          ---

          ##### Step 3 — Verify

          After validating, the invoice will be updated immediately. A green **PAID** stamp will appear on the top-right corner of the invoice form, confirming the invoice has been fully settled and the payment has been recorded in your accounting.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0006.png)

          In the case of any mistakes in the invoice, wrong delivery of products, damaged goods, or rejection of service, you can trigger a credit note to your customer. This can be done by using the **Add Credit Note** button.

          <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
            If a partial payment was registered, the invoice will show **Partially Paid** instead of
            **PAID**. You can click **Add Payment** again to register the remaining balance until the
            invoice is fully settled.
          </Noted>

          ### Add Credit Notes

          As soon as you click on this button, a new wizard will appear to define the credit note.

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/direct-credit-notes/0001.png)

           You can mention the reason to generate this credit note in the **Reason** field. The Reversal Date can be set as a Specific Date or the Journal Entry Date. The journal entry used to record the credit note can be specified in the **Use Specific Journal** field. The Refund Date will be available on the screen and you can click the **Reverse** button to reverse the payment.

           MAQSU leads you to a new window where you can observe the draft customer credit note.

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/direct-credit-notes/0002.png)

           You can click on the **Post** button to confirm the customer credit note. Later the payment can be recorded using the **Add Payment** button.

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/direct-credit-notes/0003.png)

           Once you save or post, it is automatically generate a new document number started with **RINV** and recorded inside the **Credit Notes** Menu

          ### Delete Invoices

           Deleting an invoice in this system is a simple two-step process. Here is the breakdown of how to do it:

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/delete/0001.png)

           1. **Select the Invoice**: Locate the invoice you wish to remove (for example, INV/2026/0005). Click the checkbox on the far left of the row. A checkmark will appear to indicate it is selected, as shown by the red marker (1).

           2. **Open the Action Menu**: Navigate to the top of the table and click the Action button (represented by a briefcase icon with a dropdown arrow).

           3. **Confirm Deletion**: From the dropdown menu that appears, click on Delete, as indicated by the red marker (2).

           <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
             * **Status Check**: Notice that the selected invoice has a status of "Posted." In many accounting systems, you may need to "Reset to Draft" or "Unpost" an invoice before the system will allow you to delete it permanently.
             * **Bulk Actions**: If you need to delete multiple invoices at once, you can select multiple checkboxes before clicking the Action > Delete button.
           </Noted>

           ---

           ### Print Invoice

           Printing an invoice in this system is a straightforward process. Here is the breakdown of how to do it:

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/print/0001.png)

           1. **Select the Invoice**: Locate the invoice you wish to print (for example, INV/2026/0007). Click the checkbox on the far left of the row. A checkmark will appear to indicate it is selected, as shown by the red marker (1).

           2. **Open the Print Menu**: Navigate to the top of the table and click the Print button, indicated by the red marker (2).

           3. **Choose a Format**: From the dropdown menu that appears, select your desired document format (such as Tax Invoice or Commercial Invoice), as indicated by the red marker (3).

           <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">

             * **Multi Printing**: If you need to print multiple invoices at once, you can select multiple checkboxes before clicking the Print button to generate them in a single batch.

             * **Search**: If the list is long, use the Type to search bar at the top right to quickly find a specific invoice number or reference.
           </Noted>

          ### Import Invoice

          If you have a large number of invoices to create, you can use the Import feature to upload them in bulk using a CSV or Excel file. This is especially useful when migrating data from another system or onboarding a new client with existing invoices.

          To import invoices, follow these steps:

          ---

          ##### Step 1 — Prepare Template
          First, you need to prepare your import file. You can download a template from the system that includes the required columns and formatting.

          1. *Basic Information* –  Customer, Invoice Date, Payment Terms, Journal, etc.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/import/0001.png)

          2. *Invoice Lines* –  Product, Description, Account, Quantity, UOM, Price, Discount, Taxes, etc.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/import/0002.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 2 — Import File

          From the Invoices list view, click the **Import** button at the top right of the page. Then click **Load File** to select your prepared CSV or Excel file from your computer.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/import/0003.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 3 — Choose the file

          Choose the file you want to import and click **Open**.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/import/0004.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 4 — Map Fields and Test

          After uploading, you will be prompted to map the columns in your file to the corresponding fields in the system. Ensure that all required fields are mapped correctly. You can also run a test import to check for any errors before finalizing.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/import/0005.png)

          ---

          ##### Step 5 — Import and Verify

          Once you are satisfied with the mapping and test results, click **Import** to upload all invoices. After the import is complete, verify that the invoices have been created correctly by checking the Invoices list.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/import/0006.png)

           <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
             * **Data Validation**: Before importing, ensure that your data is clean and formatted correctly to avoid errors during the import process. Use the test import feature to catch any issues early.
             * **Bulk Actions**: After importing, you can use bulk actions to post or manage the newly created invoices efficiently.
           </Noted>

          A Credit Note is an official document that reverses or reduces the amount owed from a previously posted invoice. It is used to handle refunds, billing corrections, or returned goods. In MAQSU ERP, credit notes are recorded in accounting and directly reduce the outstanding balance on the original invoice.

          ***

          ###### Step 1 — Navigation

          Navigate to **Accounting** from the left sidebar. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** ‣ **Credit Notes**.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0001.png)

          The view of this platform is the same as that of the Invoice platform. You can observe already generated credit notes here. The **Upload** button will be available to upload documents related to a credit note. For the manual creation of the Credit Note, you can click on the **Create** button

          ***

          ###### Step 2 — Create

          Click the **Create** button at the top-left to open a new blank Credit Note form.
          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0002.png)

          The Credit Notes list page will open. If no credit notes have been created yet, the page will show an empty state with the message _"The easiest way to create a credit note is to do it directly from the customer invoice."_


          ***

          ###### Step 3 — Fill Out the Credit Note Form

          The new credit note form will open in **Draft** status. Fill in the following fields:
          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0003.png)

          1. **Customer** _(Required)_ – Search and select the customer this credit note is issued for.
          2. **Reference** _(Optional)_ – Enter an internal reference or reason for the credit note (e.g., "Refund to Long Meth").
          3. **Invoice No. Ref** – Enter the original invoice number this credit note is linked to (e.g., INV/2026/0007). This links the credit note to the source invoice for traceability.
          4. **Invoice Date Ref** – Select the date of the original invoice being referenced.
          5. **Invoice Date** – The date this credit note is issued. Defaults to today.
          6. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system after saving.
          7. **Payment Terms** – The payment terms carried over from the original invoice.
          8. **Journal** – The accounting journal to post this credit note to, defaulting to **Customer Invoices (USD)**.


          Once filled in, the form will show all the entered header details.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0004.png)

          ###### Step 4 — Add Invoice Lines

          Under the **Invoice Lines** tab, add the products or services being credited. Click **Add a line** and fill in the Product, Description, Account, Quantity, UOM, Price, Discount, and Taxes for each line.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0005.png)
          The **Total Summary** at the bottom displays:

          - **Untaxed Amount** – Sum of all line subtotals before taxes.

          ***

          ###### Step 5 — Post

          After completing all fields, click **Save** to save the credit note as a Draft. Then click **Post** to officially post the credit note.

          ![](/img/accounting/credit-notes/0006.png)

          ***

          ###### Step 6 — Register Refund Payment

          1. **Journal** – Select the payment account the refund is paid from (e.g., Bank (USD)).

          3. **Amount** – Pre-filled with the credit note total (e.g., $33.26).

          5. **Memo** – Auto-populated from the credit note reference (e.g., "Refund to Long Meth").

          * **Percent**: a percentage of the invoice total.
          * **Fixed Amount**: a fixed amount in your company currency.

          * **On the**: number used to compute the due date.

          ###### Step 2 — Create

          Click the **Create** button at the top-left of the Journals list page to open a new blank journal configuration form.

          ![](/img/getting-started/journal/0003.png)

          ---

          ###### Step 3 — Basic Information

          Fill in the following required fields at the top of the form:


          ![](/img/getting-started/journal/0004.png)

          1. **Journal Name** – Enter a descriptive name for the journal (e.g., `Petty Cash Office`).
          2. **Type** – Select the journal type from the dropdown. Available types include Sales, Purchase, Cash, Bank, and Miscellaneous.
              -  **Sales**: Records all customer invoices and accounts receivable.
              -  **Purchase**: Records all vendor bills and accounts payable.
              -  **Cash & Bank**: Tracks liquid money movements (deposits, transfers, and petty cash).
              -  **Miscellaneous**: Used for non-cash entries like depreciation or internal adjustments.

          ---

          ###### Step 4 — Journal Entries

          Under the **Journal Entries** tab, configure the following:

          ![](/img/getting-started/journal/0005.png)

          1. **Short Code** – Enter a short abbreviation for the journal (e.g., `PCO`). This is used as a prefix for journal entry numbers.
          2. **Next Number** – The sequence number for the next journal entry (defaults to `1`).
          3. **Default Debit Account** – Select the default account to use for debit entries (e.g., `102002 Petty Cash Office`).
          4. **Default Credit Account** – Select the default account to use for credit entries (e.g., `102002 Petty Cash Office`).
          5. **Currency** – Select the currency for this journal (e.g., `USD`).

          ---

          ###### Step 5 — Save

          Click the **Save** button. This journal is now ready to be used in your daily accounting entries and financial reports.

          ![](/img/accounting/asset-categories/0002.png)

          ##### Step 3 — Enter Basic Information

          ![](/img/accounting/asset-categories/0003.png)

          1. **Category Name** — Enter a clear asset type name such as "Computer Equipment", "Office Furniture", or "Vehicles".

          2. **Computation Method**

              - **Straight Line** - Uses equal depreciation each period and is best for most standard business assets.
              - **Declining Balance** - Uses higher depreciation in early periods and is best for assets that lose value quickly.

          3. **Period Type**
              - **Number of Entries** - Define how many entry will generate for the full useful life.
              - **Ending Date** - Depreciate until a specific target date (useful for leases or planned end dates).
              - **Yearly** - Create one depreciation entry per year for simpler annual tracking.

          4. **Number of Depreciations** — Enter the total entry count for the full useful life (for example, 60 for 5 years x 12 months).

          5. **Period Interval** — Define how often entries are posted (for example, 1 month for monthly depreciation).

          ![](/img/accounting/asset-categories/0004.png)

          This section defines which accounting accounts will be used for assets in this category.

          1. **Journal** — Select the journal used for depreciation entries (typically "Miscellaneous Operations (USD)").

          2. **Asset Account** — Select the fixed-asset balance sheet account that records the original asset cost.

          3. **Depreciation Account** — Select the accumulated depreciation contra-asset account that reduces net asset value.

          4. **Expense Account** — Select the income-statement expense account where periodic depreciation is recognized.

          5. **Auto-Confirm Assets** — Enable to confirm assets automatically at creation time, or disable to keep manual review.

          6. **Group Journal Entries** — Enable to combine depreciation for multiple assets into one entry per period.

          7. **Depreciation Date** — Choose **Manual (Defaulted on Purchase Date)** for flexibility or **Based on Last Day of Purchase Period** for system-driven start dates.
              - **Manual (Defaulted on Purchase Date)**: Depreciation starts from the asset's purchase date, but you can adjust it when creating the asset.

              - **On Actual Run Date**: Depreciation entries are posted on the date you run the depreciation process.
