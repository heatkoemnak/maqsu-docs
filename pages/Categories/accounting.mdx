    body: |
      Accounts receivable (AR) refers to the money owed to a business for goods or services that have been delivered but not yet paid for, and it is recorded as a current asset on the company's balance sheet.

      In MAQSU, Accounts Receivable is the account that tracks money customers owe you from unpaid customer invoices. When you create and confirm a customer invoice, MAQSU treats that amount as receivable until it is paid and reconciled.

      ![](/img/accounting/receivable/0001.png)

      <Noted type="info" title="Key Takeaway" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
      * Accounts receivable (AR) is an item on a company's balance sheet that represents money due the company for products or services it has already delivered.
      * Accounts receivable is considered an asset to the company.
      * The opposite of accounts receivable is accounts payable, which reflects money that a company owes but has not yet paid.
      </Noted>

          An **Invoice** is an official document sent to a customer that details goods or services provided, their quantities, unit prices, taxes, and the total amount due. In MAQSU ERP, invoices are crucial for tracking sales, managing customer payments, and ensuring proper financial records.

          There are several methods available in MAQSU that automate customer invoice creation depending on the operation you perform and the module you use. While discussing the **Sales** module, we mentioned the management of invoices. Once you confirm a sales order, you will get a draft customer invoice in MAQSU. You just need to validate the draft invoices and send them to the respective customers. All such automated invoices will be recorded and managed in the Invoices platform available under the **Customers** menu of the **Accounting** module. This platform will allow you to create invoices manually also.

          From the left sidebar, click **Accounting**. Then from the top navigation menu, go to **Customers** ‣ **Invoices**.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0001.png)

          Once you navigate to **Invoices**, you will see the master list of all existing customer invoices. The list displays the following columns:

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0002.png)

           1. **Number** – The unique system-generated invoice identifier following the format **INV/YEAR/SEQUENCE** (e.g., INV/2026/0001, INV/2026/0006). Automatically assigned when an invoice is created.

           2. **Reference** – An optional field for adding external reference numbers, such as a purchase order (PO) number or any internal reference code from the customer. All invoices here have no reference filled in.

           3. **Invoice Date** – The official date the invoice was issued to the customer. All invoices here are dated **20/03/2026**.

           4. **Customer** – The name of the customer the invoice was billed to (e.g., Sin Piseth, LY CHAIMOUY, Seng Somaly, Mr. Bunleng, Chay Sokly).

           5. **Due Date** – The deadline by which the customer must complete payment. All invoices here share the same due date **20/03/2026**, matching the invoice date.

           6. **Total** – The full invoice amount including all products, services, and taxes (e.g., $8.99, $5.50, $1,524.60, $132.00).

           7. **Amount Due** – The **remaining balance** still owed by the customer. Shows **$0.00** for fully paid invoices (e.g., INV/2026/0005 – Mr. Bunleng) and the outstanding amount for unpaid ones.

           8. **Status** – The current processing stage of the invoice:
             - **Posted** – Invoice has been confirmed and sent (e.g., INV/2026/0001, INV/2026/0004)
             - **Draft** – Invoice has been created but not yet confirmed (e.g., INV/2026/0003 – Seng Somaly, shown in blue)

           9. **Payment** – Indicates whether the invoice has been settled:
             - **Paid** – Payment has been received in full (e.g., INV/2026/0005 – Mr. Bunleng, $0.00 remaining)
             - **Not Paid** – Payment is still outstanding (e.g., INV/2026/0001 – Sin Piseth, $8.99 still due)

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/0003.png)

          Using the default **Filters**, you can filter the invoices based on My Invoices, Draft, Posted, Canceled, To Check, Unpaid, Paid, Overdue, Invoice Date, and Due Date. You are allowed to create custom filters for invoices using the Add Custom Filter button.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/0004.png)

          In the **Group By** option, you can group the available invoices according to the Salesperson, Status, Sales Team, Electronic Invoicing State, Preferred Payment Method, Invoice Date, Due Date, and Referrer. The Add Custom Group button helps you to create custom group options.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/0005.png)

           From this menu, you can enable or disable required fields for the list view of the customer invoice. It includes the Invoice Date, Due Date, Source Document, Reference, Salesperson, Sales Team, Activities, Company, Tax Excluded, Tax, Total, Total in Currency, Amount Due, Invoice Currency, To Check, Payment Status, Electronic Invoicing, and Status.

          ### Create Invoice

          Let's create a new customer invoice by following steps down below:

          ###### Step 1 — Create

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0003.png)
          Click the **Create** button at the top-left of the Invoices list page to open a new blank invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0004.png)

          ###### Step 2 — Basic information

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0005.png)

          The new invoice form will open in **Draft** status. Fill in the following required and optional fields:
          1. **Customer** _(Required)_ – Search and select the customer by name, code, email, or reference. Start typing and select the correct entry from the dropdown list. Let's see how to setup [Cusomters](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#customer-vendor)
          2. **Reference** _(Optional)_ – Enter an internal reference number or note to help identify this invoice. This is useful for linking the invoice to a sales order or project.
          3. **Invoice Date** – The date the invoice is issued. This defaults to today's date but can be adjusted.
          4. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system after saving. You do not need to fill this in manually.
          5. **Payment Terms** – Select the agreed payment timeframe or conditions (e.g., 15 Days, Due on Receipt). This determines the due date shown on the invoice. Let's see how to setup [Payment Terms](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#payment-terms)
          6. **Journal** – The accounting journal where the invoice will be posted. This defaults to **Customer Invoices (USD)**. Let's see how to setup [Journals](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#journals)
          7. **Pricelist** _(Optional)_ – Select a pricelist to automatically apply special pricing or discounts for the customer. The selected pricelist will update product prices on the invoice lines accordingly.

          Once you fill in the customer and other header details, the form will update to show the entered information.


          ###### Step 3 — Add products or services

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0006.png)

          Under the **Invoice Lines** tab, add the products or services you are invoicing the customer for. Click **Add a line** to add a new line item, then fill in the following fields for each line:

          1. **Product** – Choose the product or service being invoiced. Start typing the product name and select the correct entry from the list of created products in **Inventory > Products**. This will auto-populate the description, account, and price. For more information on creating products, see how to [Create Product Guide](/getting-started/setup-master-datas#products).
          2. **Description** – Optionally modify or add a detailed description for the item. This appears on the printed invoice sent to the customer.
          3. **Account** – The accounting account used to classify this revenue line (e.g., 401100 Sales of Goods). This determines how the sale is tracked in financial reports.
          4. **Quantity** – The number of units being invoiced. The subtotal updates automatically.
          5. **UOM** – The unit of measure for the item (e.g., Units, Can, kg).
          6. **Price** – The unit price per item. This may auto-fill from the product or pricelist but can be adjusted.
          7. **Discount** – Enter a percentage discount to apply on this line. The subtotal will update to reflect the discount.
          8. **Taxes** – Select the applicable taxes for the product (e.g., VAT - Sales of Goods 10%). The tax amount is calculated and included in the total.
          9. **Subtotal** – Automatically calculated as: **Subtotal = (Quantity x Price) – Discount**.

          You can also use **Add a section** to group related items, or **Add a note** to include internal remarks on a specific line.

          At the bottom of the Invoice Lines section, the **Total Summary** shows:

          - **Untaxed Amount** – The sum of all line subtotals before taxes.
          - **VAT** – The total tax amount applied.
          - **Total** – The grand total due including taxes.

          ###### Step 5 — Save

          <Noted type="info" title="Note" image="/img/icon/notes.png">
            Carefully verify that all amounts are correct. If any discrepancies are found, review and
            adjust the corresponding invoice lines or tax settings before continuing.
          </Noted>

          After completing all required fields, click **Save** to save the invoice as a **Draft**. At this stage the invoice is not yet posted to accounting  -  you can still edit it.

          ###### Step 6 — Post

          This Invoice Form currently in **Draft** status. So once you have reviewed and confirmed all details are correct, click the **Post** button to officially post the invoice. This will:

          - Change the invoice status from **Draft** to **Posted** – Assign a permanent invoice number (e.g., INV/2026/0007)
          - Record the corresponding journal entries in accounting

          ![](/img/accounting/invoices/create/0007.png)

          After posting, the invoice is locked for editing. You will see the **Add Payment**, **Add Credit Note**, and **Reset to Draft** buttons at the top of the form. Let's see how to add payment for the posted cusotmer invoice [Add Payment](#account-receivable-invoices-addpayment).

          <Noted type="info" title="Note" image="/img/icon/notes.png">
            If you notice an error after posting, click **Reset to Draft** to bring the invoice back to
            Draft status for editing, then re-post it once corrections are made.
          </Noted>

          ### Add Payment

          Once the customer gets this invoice, they can check the details and pay the invoice. So now you can record their payment using the **Add Payment** button.

          ###### Step 1 — Click add payment

          On the posted invoice, click the **Add Payment** button at the top of the invoice form.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0004.png)

          ###### Step 2 — Add payment detials

          MAQSU generates a pop-up window to confirm the details of the payment registration. The window will show the:

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0005.png)

          1. **Journal** – Select the payment account where the money is received (e.g., Bank (USD), Cash). This determines which financial account is credited.
          2. **Doc. No.** – Automatically generated by the system. Leave as is or enter a custom reference such as a bank transfer ID or check number.
          3. **Amount** – Pre-filled with the outstanding invoice balance. Adjust for partial payments if needed.
          4. **Payment Date** – The date the payment was received from the customer. Defaults to today.
          5. **Memo** – Auto-populated with the invoice number for reference (e.g., INV/2026/0007).

          You can make changes to the available details if needed. Now, click the **Validate** button. The payment registration can be completed by this step. Once the customer pays the invoice, the status of the invoice can be observed as Paid.

          ###### Step 3 — Verify the Invoice

          After validating, the invoice will be updated immediately. A green **PAID** stamp will appear on the top-right corner of the invoice form, confirming the invoice has been fully settled and the payment has been recorded in your accounting.

          ![](/img/accounting/payments/0006.png)

          In the case of any mistakes in the invoice, wrong delivery of products, damaged goods, or rejection of service, you can trigger a credit note to your customer. This can be done by using the **Add Credit Note** button.

          <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
            If a partial payment was registered, the invoice will show **Partially Paid** instead of
            **PAID**. You can click **Add Payment** again to register the remaining balance until the
            invoice is fully settled.
          </Noted>

           ### Delete Invoices

           Deleting an invoice in this system is a simple two-step process. Here is the breakdown of how to do it:

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/delete/0001.png)

           1. **Select the Invoice**: Locate the invoice you wish to remove (for example, INV/2026/0005). Click the checkbox on the far left of the row. A checkmark will appear to indicate it is selected, as shown by the red marker (1).

           2. **Open the Action Menu**: Navigate to the top of the table and click the Action button (represented by a briefcase icon with a dropdown arrow).

           3. **Confirm Deletion**: From the dropdown menu that appears, click on Delete, as indicated by the red marker (2).

           <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">
             * **Status Check**: Notice that the selected invoice has a status of "Posted." In many accounting systems, you may need to "Reset to Draft" or "Unpost" an invoice before the system will allow you to delete it permanently.
             * **Bulk Actions**: If you need to delete multiple invoices at once, you can select multiple checkboxes before clicking the Action > Delete button.
           </Noted>

           ### Print Invoice

           Printing an invoice in this system is a straightforward process. Here is the breakdown of how to do it:

           ![](/img/accounting/invoices/print/0001.png)

           1. **Select the Invoice**: Locate the invoice you wish to print (for example, INV/2026/0007). Click the checkbox on the far left of the row. A checkmark will appear to indicate it is selected, as shown by the red marker (1).

           2. **Open the Print Menu**: Navigate to the top of the table and click the Print button, indicated by the red marker (2).

           3. **Choose a Format**: From the dropdown menu that appears, select your desired document format (such as Tax Invoice or Commercial Invoice), as indicated by the red marker (3).

           <Noted type="info" title="Tip" image="/img/icon/tip.png">

          * **Mark invoice as fully paid** — Writes off the $66 difference; closes the invoice

          Here is a detailed comparison of both options:Here is the detailed explanation of each option:

          ---

          **Keep open** — choose this when the customer genuinely owes you the remaining $66 and will pay it later. After you validate:
          - The invoice stays open and shows as partially paid
          - MAQSU tracks that $66 is still receivable (the customer still owes it)
          - You can go back to the same invoice and click "Add Payment" again to record the second payment whenever the customer pays
          - Your accounts receivable balance still includes the $66

          - The invoice is permanently closed and marked as Paid
          - MAQSU posts the $66 difference to a write-off account (usually a loss or discount expense account)
          - You cannot go back to collect the $66 anymore — the invoice is done
          - Use this for situations like: giving a discount after the fact, forgiving a small balance, or recording a bad debt that the customer cannot pay

          ---

          <Noted type="info" title="In short" image="/img/icon/tip.png">

          - Reversing entry neutralizes the effect on accounts

          **Deferred expenses** and prepayments (also known as prepaid expenses) are both costs that have already occurred for products or services yet to be received.

          Such costs are **assets** for the company that pays them since it already paid for products and services but has either not yet received them or not yet used them.
